# The problem

## Problem statement

When people walk through an **unfamiliar** area at night, they have no easy way to know which way feels safer. Navigation apps optimise for speed only, and the local knowledge that matters (which streets are well lit, where places stay open) is scattered across friends and private group chats.

## Who has it

- **The newcomer** *(early adopter)*: new to the city, so every route is unfamiliar. Easiest group to reach first (universities, student associations, residences).
- **The night-out visitor** *(largest group)*: lives nearby but doesn’t know that part of town; walking back from dinner, an event, or to the car or metro.

## How people deal with it today

- Asking friends which way to go
- Walking fast while on the phone with someone
- Paying for a ride they hadn’t planned for
- Avoiding going out, or leaving early

## Why now

People already share their location with friends in many apps, and maps now include useful information about streets and places. What’s missing is a way to bring that together into a simple, explained choice between routes.

## What success looks like

- **North Star:** safe walks completed per week (walk started, then arrival confirmed)
- **Supporting signals:** how often the safer route is chosen when it differs from the fastest; whether people felt safe on the walk; whether they come back for a second walk
- **Guardrail:** the extra time of the safer route stays small
